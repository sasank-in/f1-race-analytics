Use regression-based estimation of the fuel sensitivity coefficient. This is the best approach if you want a reliable F1 lap-time correction rather than using a fixed 0.03 s/kg.

Recommended methodology

Model each clean lap as:

$$ \boxed{ LapTime = \beta_0 +\beta_f FuelMass +\beta_t TyreAge +\beta_{t2} TyreAge^2 +\beta_c Compound +\beta_s TrackStatus +\epsilon } $$

The coefficient you want is:

$$ \boxed{\beta_f = \text{seconds lost per kg of fuel}} $$
Implementation
Collect lap-by-lap data:
lap time
lap number
tyre compound
tyre age
pit stops
safety-car/VSC status
race distance
Estimate fuel mass:
$$ FuelMass_i = FuelStart - FuelBurnPerLap \times (Lap_i-1) $$
Remove abnormal laps:
pit-in/out
Safety Car/VSC
red flags
major traffic incidents
obvious driver errors
Fit the regression model.
Extract β_f.

For example, if the model gives:

$$ \beta_f=0.032 $$

then use:

$$ \boxed{ FuelCorrection_i=0.032\times FuelMass_i } $$
